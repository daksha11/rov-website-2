## First: What Is GEO?

**SEO** = getting Google to rank your page when someone types a search query.
**GEO** = getting AI search engines (ChatGPT Search, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews) to *cite* your page when someone asks a question.

The difference matters because AI search is changing how people find businesses:
- Old: type "web design Atlanta" → click a link → visit site
- New: ask "who's a good web designer for my Atlanta restaurant?" → AI gives an answer with citations → user clicks the citation

If AI doesn't know you exist or doesn't trust your page as a source, you don't get cited. Someone else does.

**AEO (Answer Engine Optimization)** = a subset of GEO. Specifically optimizing for featured snippets and voice search — the "quick answer" boxes Google shows above results.

```
  H3: Sub-questions within each section

↓
FAQ section (H3s phrased as questions + paragraph answers)
↓
"External sources I cite and trust" section

↓

```

### Why it works for GEO:
1. **H1 is the search query itself** — "What Does a Neurodiversity Program Actually Cost?" An AI answering "how much does neurodiversity training cost?" will cite this because the headline matches the question exactly.
2. **Author with credentials shown** — "Debra Solomon, NYU-Certified Life & Career Coach, 30+ years experience." AI engines weight authority. A named expert beats "ROV Studios Team."
3. **External sources section** — Shows the AI that this page is connected to other authoritative sources, not an island.
4. **FAQ schema** — Each FAQ Q&A is a direct citation opportunity. AI engines pull these verbatim.
5. **Statistics with specifics** — "six to nine months of salary" — specific, citable, memorable.
6. **2,200+ words** — Depth signals authority. Short pages don't get cited.

### What to note about the date:
Updated June 28, 2026 — one day before we studied it. GEO rewards freshness. An AI engine checking sources will prefer a page updated last week over one from 2022.

## The Rules of a Good GEO Page

### MUST HAVES

**1. H1 = the exact question someone asks**
Not "Atlanta Web Design Services" — that's SEO from 2015.
Good: "How Much Does a Website Cost for an Atlanta Small Business?"
Good: "What's the Best Way to Get More Leads for an Atlanta HVAC Company?"
Good: "Why Isn't My Atlanta Business Showing Up on Google?"

**2. Named author + date always visible**
- Author name (not "ROV Team" — a real person: Suchet Konda, Ayush Basu)
- Author role/credentials (Co-Founder & Systems Architect, Founder & Creative Director)
- Last updated date (keep it fresh — update at least every 90 days)

**3. Specific numbers with context**
Every claim needs a number:
- Not: "We helped TheBando get more orders"
- Yes: "TheBando's online ordering page went from 132 views to 91,000 in 139 days"
- Not: "We can help your business grow"
- Yes: "The average Atlanta home services company loses 4 calls per week to missed calls that go unanswered"

**4. FAQ section with schema**
Minimum 4-6 questions. Each answer: 50-100 words. Direct. The question should be how a real human would phrase it.
- "How much does a website cost for a small business in Atlanta?"
- "How long does it take to build a business website?"
- "Can AI automations really help a small business?"

**5. Table of contents with anchor links**
AI engines use this to understand page structure. Readers use it to jump. Both benefit.

**6. "Sources" or "Further Reading" section**
Link to 2-3 authoritative external sources. Shows you're not an island. AI engines trust pages that cite other trusted sources.

**7. 1,500+ words**
Short pages don't get cited. The depth signals authority. This doesn't mean padding — it means actually answering the question completely.

### NICE TO HAVE

- Summary box ("The short answer is...") near the top — AI pulls this for quick answers
- Internal links to related ROV pages and case studies
- Pull quotes or callout boxes highlighting key stats
- "Last reviewed by" note if updated by someone other than the author
- HowTo sections formatted as numbered steps (Google pulls these for featured snippets)

### WHAT NOT TO DO

- Don't write for Google bots — write for the human who asked the question, and the AI will follow
- Don't keyword stuff the H1 — "Best Atlanta Web Design Agency Services Small Business 2026" is spam, not a headline
- Don't skip the author — anonymous pages get lower trust scores from AI engines
- Don't be vague — "great results" and "industry-leading" get ignored; "689x in 139 days" gets cited
- Don't write once and forget — update the date every time you refresh the content
