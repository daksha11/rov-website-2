Short version: expect somewhere between $40 and $200 a song to have it mixed and mastered in Atlanta, depending on who you go to and how many songs you bring. Studio time to actually record is a separate cost, and Atlanta rooms average around $102 an hour.

The longer version matters more, because the reason you had to search for this at all is that almost nobody in this city will tell you a number.

## Why nobody publishes their rates

We checked the sites of the best known studios in Atlanta. Patchwerk, SING Mastering, Solar Sound, Meadowlark. Not one of them lists a price. Every single one is quote only.

That is not a scam and it is not unusual. There are real reasons for it. Sessions genuinely vary, a three-piece band tracking live is a different job from one vocalist over a beat, and quoting per project keeps negotiating room for work that is genuinely hard to scope in advance.

But it has a cost, and independent artists pay it. If you are making your first record and you cannot find a single number anywhere, you cannot build a budget. You cannot decide between eight hours of studio time and four songs mixed. You end up either overspending on the wrong stage or not starting at all.

So here are real numbers, including ours.

## What the market actually looks like

**Studio time in Atlanta** averages roughly $102 an hour according to studio rental marketplaces, with budget listings closer to $35 to $55. That range is wide because it is measuring very different things: a large SSL tracking room with a live space and an engineer is not the same product as an hourly rental booth.

**Mixing and mastering per song** is harder to pin down publicly because it is nearly all quote based. In practice the market splits into roughly three tiers:

- **Entry, roughly $40 to $75 a song.** Independent engineers and small studios. This is where a first release belongs.
- **Established, roughly $100 to $400 a song.** Studios with a track record and engineers whose time is in demand.
- **Specialist, quote only and considerably higher.** Mastering houses working on major label records. Genuinely excellent and genuinely not what a first single needs.

A more expensive studio is usually a bigger studio. Rate mostly reflects the room, the console, and demand for the engineer's time. A large tracking room is worth every dollar for a full band with a producer, and completely wasted on one vocalist recording to a beat.

The "mix and master included" part is the bit worth reading twice. At most studios, hourly time buys you the room and the recording. The mix is a separate line item afterward. Ours is not, which is why comparing our hourly rate directly against a cheaper hourly rate elsewhere is usually comparing two different products.

## Where first records actually lose money

Four mistakes, in rough order of how much they cost.

**Booking studio time to write in.** The studio is the most expensive place in the world to figure out your arrangement. Every room in this city charges for the hour whether you are recording or deciding. Demo at home, arrive with the song settled.

**Paying for a room you are not using.** If you already have usable takes, you need mixing, not studio time, and stems can be sent to anyone anywhere. Booking a live room for a session that is one vocalist and a laptop is the most common way a first budget disappears.

**Running the stages out of order.** Paying for a master, hearing something is still wrong, paying for a remix, then needing a new master because the mix changed. Three invoices for one record. We wrote about [why mastering cannot fix a bad mix](https://www.rovmusic.com/blog/can-mastering-fix-a-bad-mix) separately, because this one is expensive and extremely common.

**Buying plugins instead of skill.** Stock plugins in Logic or Pro Tools get you roughly ninety percent of the way. We have finished released records on stock chains. A plugin folder is not a mix.

## What to ask before you book anywhere

You are allowed to ask for the number in writing. Quote only pricing is normal, but a studio that will not give you a figure before you commit is telling you something.

Four questions worth asking any engineer, us included:

1. Is mixing included in the hourly rate, or billed separately?
2. How many revisions are included, and what counts as a revision?
3. What turnaround should I expect on a first pass?
4. Can I hear a record you mixed that sounds like what I am going for?

That last one matters more than any rate. Anyone can describe a sound. Ask to hear it. Ours are on [our credits page](https://www.rovmusic.com/credits), every one named and linked so you can go listen instead of taking our word for it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does it cost to mix a song in Atlanta?

Roughly $40 to $200 a song depending on the studio and how many songs you bring. Independent engineers and small studios sit around $40 to $75, established studios around $100 to $400, and specialist mastering houses considerably higher. Our published rate is $50 for a first song, $65 for a single song after that, and down to $40 a song on a twelve song pack.

### How much is studio time in Atlanta?

Studio rental marketplaces list Atlanta rooms averaging around $102 an hour, with budget listings closer to $35 to $55. Our studio time is $65 an hour with mix and master included, or $200 for a four hour block, which works out at $50 an hour.

### Why do Atlanta recording studios not list their prices?

Quote based pricing is the industry norm. Sessions genuinely vary and quoting per project keeps negotiating room. It is not dishonest, but it does make budgeting a first record very difficult, which is why we publish ours.

### Is it cheaper to mix online than to book a studio in Atlanta?

Usually, because you are not paying for the room. Mixing and mastering are remote by default and stems can be sent from anywhere. Being in Atlanta only matters when you need the room itself, meaning tracking, or when you want the arrangement conversation to happen in person.

### How many songs should I mix at once?

If you have them ready, batching is meaningfully cheaper almost everywhere, ours included: $65 for a single song against $40 a song across twelve. But only batch songs that are genuinely finished. Paying a lower per song rate on tracks that still need writing is not a saving.

### What do I need to send to get a song mixed?

Consolidated stems starting from bar one, named plainly, with no printed effects unless the effect is part of the performance. Two or three reference tracks that sound like what you want. A note on what is bothering you about the current version. Mislabeled or unconsolidated stems are the most common reason a first pass takes longer than it should.
