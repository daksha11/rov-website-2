# Cold Call · Variant A · The Diagnostic

**Mechanism being tested:** permission + a real, specific observation. Low pressure. The rep is a specialist noticing a fixable problem, not a salesperson.
**Caller:** hired SDR / rep (speaks on behalf of ROV, name-drops the studio and founder).
**Goal:** book the 15-minute call live, on this call.
**Destination offer:** free site teardown → 15-min call → The 14-Day Booking Site.

Voice: calm, curious, low-pressure. If they're busy, get off fast and earn the callback. No hype, no fake urgency.

---

## Before you dial
1. Open their site on your phone. Find **one** true, specific leak. Examples:
   - Booking/call button is below the fold or hard to tap.
   - Site takes 5+ seconds to load on mobile.
   - No way to book online, phone-only.
   - No reviews or photos above the fold.
2. Write that one observation at the top of your card. You lead with it. Never call without it.

---

## The script

**Opener — permission + who you are:**
> Hi, is this [name]? Hey [name], I'm [rep] calling from Range of View, we're a studio that builds websites for [their industry] here in [city]. I'll be straight with you, this is a cold call, but it's a quick one. Got 30 seconds?

*(If no: "No problem, when's a better time to catch you for two minutes?" Get a time, hang up.)*

**Hook — the observation (this is the whole opener, make it real):**
> Appreciate it. So before I called I pulled up your site, and I noticed [the one specific leak]. That's usually the kind of thing that quietly costs a [business like yours] bookings every month. Is getting more customers through your website something you're thinking about right now, or are you mostly full from referrals?

*(The "or mostly full from referrals" gives them an honest out and surfaces the ones who actually want more business. If they're full and happy: "Love that, that's the goal. I'll let you go, and if it ever changes we're here." Hang up warm.)*

**Diagnostic — two questions, get the math inputs:**
> Got it. Two quick questions so I'm not wasting your time. Roughly how many people land on your site in a month, ballpark? ... And what's a new [customer] worth to you, on average?

*(You now have visitors and value. Do the math out loud.)*
> Okay, so even being conservative, if we took your site from around 2 in 100 visitors booking to about 5 in 100, that's roughly [X] more [customers] a month for you. At $[value] each, that's about $[Y] a month you're leaving on the table. That's the whole reason I called.

**Offer — the low-commitment yes:**
> Here's what I'd suggest. Our founder does a short teardown, a quick video walking through the top 3 things we'd fix on your site to get you there. Costs you nothing. But instead of me just emailing it into the void, the way it actually works is we hop on a quick 15-minute call, walk you through it live, and you decide from there. No obligation.

**Close — book it live (this is the goal):**
> I've got [day] at [time] or [day] at [time] open, which works better for you?

*(Assume the close. Offer two specific slots, not "are you free sometime.")*

**Lock it in:**
> Perfect, I'll send a calendar invite. Best email for that? ... Great. On that call we'll have the teardown of your actual site ready, so it's 15 minutes well spent. Talk [day].

---

## Objection handling

**"We already have a website."**
> Totally, everyone I call does. The question isn't whether you have one, it's whether it's booking customers or just sitting there looking nice. That's exactly what the teardown shows you. Fifteen minutes, worst case you find out it's fine.

**"How much does it cost?"**
> The rebuilds run between $2k and $10k, one time, not monthly, depending on scope. But honestly the price only matters if the math works, and that's what the call is for. If the extra bookings don't cover it easily, I'd tell you not to do it. Let's just get the teardown in front of you first. [day] or [day]?

**"I'm too busy right now."**
> Completely fair, that's actually why the call's only 15 minutes and we do the work before it, not during. When's your quietest day this week, [day] morning or [day] afternoon?

**"Just email me some info."**
> I can do better than generic info, we'll put together a teardown of *your* site specifically. But it lands way better when our founder walks you through it than as an attachment you never open. It's 15 minutes. [day] at [time] work?

**"How'd you get my number?"**
> You're listed publicly as a [industry] in [city], that's the whole list I'm working from, local businesses whose sites I think are leaving money on the table. Yours stood out because of [the leak]. Happy to show you on a quick call.

**"Not interested."**
> Fair enough, I'll get out of your hair. Can I ask, is it that the website's just not a priority right now, or that you've been burned by a web person before? [Listen.] Either way, no pressure. I'll leave you my name in case it changes.

---

## After the call
- **Booked:** send the calendar invite within 5 minutes while you're still fresh in their mind. Confirm the teardown will be ready.
- **Soft no / callback:** log the time they gave you and actually call back then. Showing up when you said you would is half the trust.
- **Hard no:** mark it, move on. No chasing.

## What to log for the A/B/C test
Track these four numbers per variant so the test means something:
```
Dials:              ______
Conversations:      ______   (actually reached a decision-maker)
Calls booked:       ______
Show / close:       ______
```
Booked ÷ Conversations is the number that decides A vs B vs C.
