# ROV Local-Service Sales Playbook

Target: local service businesses (restaurants, gyms, med spas, dentists, real estate, home services).
Lead offer: website / landing page revamp, tied to revenue.
Deal size: $2k–$10k projects.

Voice: Grounded, Warm, Refined. No hype, no fake urgency, no "limited spots." We sell outcomes, not adjectives.

---

## 0. Why the current funnel probably isn't converting

Most agency funnels for this audience sell *design*: "custom websites," "beautiful," "modern," "we build brands." A local owner doesn't lie awake wanting a beautiful site. They want a fuller calendar. Every asset below makes one shift:

**Stop selling the site. Sell the booked jobs the site brings in, and prove the math before you ask for money.**

Three levers that fix conversion:
1. **Revenue framing** · every claim ties to their money, not our craft.
2. **Time to value** · they see proof (a free teardown) before they pay, and the build is fast enough to feel real.
3. **Risk reversal** · we carry the risk with a measurable guarantee, so "yes" costs them nothing but a call.

---

## 1. The Offer: "The 14-Day Booking Site"

The offer is the whole game. A named, specific, revenue-tied offer converts 3–5x better than "we build websites."

**The pitch, one breath:**
> We rebuild your site in 14 days around a single job: turning the people who already find you into booked customers. If it doesn't lift your bookings in 60 days, we keep working until it does. Free.

### What it is
- A rebuilt site or dedicated landing page, live in **14 days**.
- Built around **one conversion goal**: booked table, booked appointment, quote request, membership signup, whichever is their money moment.
- The three things local sites almost always get wrong, fixed: **speed** (loads in under 2s on mobile), **one clear action above the fold**, **proof** (reviews, photos, trust signals where they matter).
- Tracking installed so the lift is **measured, not claimed**.

### Time to value (the wedge)
- **Day 0:** free teardown call, they see exactly what's leaking.
- **Day 1–14:** we build.
- **Day 14:** live.
- **Day 15–60:** the numbers move, we watch them together.

Fast enough that the owner feels momentum. That momentum is what closes the next referral.

### The guarantee (risk reversal)
> Measurable lift in bookings within 60 days, or we keep optimizing at no additional cost until you get it.

Pick the version that fits the deal:
- **Soft (default):** we keep working free until the number moves.
- **Harder (for confident fits):** hit the agreed lift or refund the build fee. Only offer this when the teardown shows obvious, fixable leaks.

### Price framing
Never say a number cold. Anchor it against what one job is worth to them first.

> A new [customer] is worth about $[avg ticket] to you, and more over their lifetime. The site runs $[2–10k] once. So the question isn't the price, it's how many extra [customers] it takes to pay for itself. Usually that's [3–8].

### The revenue math you show on every call
This one napkin calc does more selling than any portfolio. Fill it in live with their real numbers:

```
Monthly site visitors:            ______   (pull from their analytics or estimate)
Current booking rate:             ~2%      (typical for a leaky local site)
Current bookings/mo:              ______
Average customer value:           $______

After rebuild, booking rate:      ~5%      (conservative for a focused rebuild)
New bookings/mo:                  ______
Extra bookings/mo:                ______
Extra revenue/mo:                 $______
Site pays for itself in:          ______ weeks
```

When they watch you turn their own traffic into a dollar figure, the price stops being an expense and becomes a rounding error.

## 2. The Instagram Funnel

Goal: turn strangers into a booked teardown call. The funnel is content → DM keyword → free teardown → call → close. No hard selling in the feed. The content *is* the proof.

### Stage 1 · Content that earns the DM

Post formats, in priority order (these do the heaviest lifting for local service):

1. **Teardowns.** Screen-record a real local business site (anonymized or with permission) and narrate the 3 things costing them bookings. Ends with: "Want yours? Comment AUDIT." This single format is the engine.
2. **Before / after.** Old site → new site, with the number that moved. "This gym's signups went from 11 to 34 a month. Same traffic. Different site."
3. **One-fix reels.** 20 seconds, one specific fix. "Your phone number should be a tap-to-call button. Here's why you're losing the 60-year-old customer." Positions ROV as the person who actually knows local.
4. **Proof in passing.** A client texting "we're slammed this week," a review, a booking screenshot. Quiet flex, per brand voice.

Cadence: 3–4 posts/week, at least half of them teardown or before/after. Consistency beats production value here.

### Stage 2 · The DM trigger

CTA on every relevant post: **"Comment AUDIT and I'll send you a free 3-minute teardown of your site."**

Keyword ("AUDIT") does two things: it's a low-commitment yes, and it lets you automate the first reply.

### Stage 3 · The DM script

**Auto / first reply (on keyword):**
> Hey! Happy to do this. Drop your website link here and I'll record a quick teardown of the top 3 things costing you bookings. No pitch, just the honest read.

**After they send the link (do this by hand, it's worth it):**
Record a real 2–3 minute Loom of their actual site. Point at real leaks. Send it with:
> Here's your teardown [link]. The biggest one is [specific leak], that's the one I'd fix first. If you want, I'll walk you through how we'd rebuild it around bookings on a quick call. Free either way, the teardown's yours to keep.

**The booking nudge:**
> Easiest is a 15-min call, I'll show you the numbers on what fixing these is worth. Here's my calendar: [link]. Or if you'd rather, tell me your average customer value and I'll ballpark it right here.

### Why this converts
- The free teardown is real value delivered *before* the ask. Reciprocity does the closing.
- Their own site on screen makes the problem undeniable.
- "Free either way" removes the flinch.
- You never pitch a stranger, you diagnose a warm lead.

## 3. The Cold Call Script

Cold calls fail when they pitch. This one **diagnoses**. You are a specialist noticing a problem, not a salesperson reciting features. Tone: calm, curious, low-pressure. If they're busy, you get off the phone fast and earn the callback.

### The frame
You looked at their site before calling. You have one specific, true observation. Lead with it.

### The script

**Opener (permission + specificity):**
> Hi, is this [name]? Hey [name], my name's [you] with Range of View, we build websites for [their industry] here in [city]. I'll be honest, this is a cold call, but it's a quick one. Got 30 seconds?

*(Asking permission disarms. It's warm, not slippery.)*

**The hook (real observation, not a pitch):**
> I was looking at your site before I called, and I noticed [specific true thing: "on my phone your booking button is below three scrolls" / "it takes about 6 seconds to load, which is where you lose most mobile visitors" / "there's no way to book without calling"]. Is getting more [bookings/customers] through the site something you're even thinking about right now, or are you mostly full from referrals?

*(That last clause gives them an easy out and makes you honest. It also surfaces the ones who actually want more business.)*

**If interested · the diagnostic (ask, don't tell):**
> Got it. Two quick questions so I'm not wasting your time. Roughly how many people land on your site a month, ballpark? ... And what's a new [customer] worth to you, roughly?

*(Now you have the math inputs. Do the calc out loud.)*
> So even at a conservative read, if we lifted your site from around 2% booking to 5%, that's about [X] extra [customers] a month, call it $[Y] a month. That's the whole reason I called.

**The offer (low commitment):**
> Here's what I'd suggest, no cost: let me record a short teardown of your site, the top 3 things I'd fix to get you there. You watch it on your own time, and if it's useful we talk about a rebuild. If not, you keep the teardown and I'll leave you alone. Fair?

**The close (book the next step):**
> Great, what's the best email or number to send it to? ... And I'll grab 15 minutes on your calendar for later this week to walk through it, does [day] or [day] work better?

### Objection handling

**"We already have a website."**
> Totally, most people I call do. The question isn't whether you have one, it's whether it's booking customers or just sitting there. That's exactly what the free teardown shows. Worst case you learn it's fine.

**"How much does this cost?"**
> Depends on scope, but our rebuilds run between $2k and $10k, once, not monthly. Before we get near that though, let me show you what it's worth in extra bookings. If the math doesn't work, the price is irrelevant. Let me send the teardown first.

**"I'm too busy / not interested."**
> Completely fair, I'll be quick then and get out of your hair. Can I just send you the free teardown by email? No call, no follow-up unless you ask. You look at it whenever. Worth a shot?

**"Just send me some info."**
> Better than info, I'll send something specific to *your* site, not a brochure. Best email? And if the teardown's useful, you tell me. Deal?

**"Does it really work / prove it."**
> Fair to be skeptical. That's the point of the teardown, it's free and it's about your site, not a case study about someone else's. You judge for yourself before any money changes hands.

### After the call
- Send the teardown within 24 hours, while the call is warm.
- One follow-up if they go quiet: "Did the teardown make sense? Happy to walk through the [specific leak] one whenever."
- Then stop. No chasing. Per brand voice, we don't fake urgency.

## 4. How the three pieces work together

```
Instagram content ──┐
                     ├──► Free site teardown ──► 15-min call ──► Booking Site offer ──► Close
Cold call ───────────┘
```

Both channels funnel to the **same free teardown**, which funnels to the **same call**, which lands the **same offer**. One message, three doors. That consistency is what compounds: the reel makes the cold call warmer, the cold call makes the next referral easier.

### First 30 days to test it
1. Build the teardown template (a repeatable 3-minute Loom structure) so you can turn one around in 10 minutes.
2. Post 3 teardown/before-after pieces a week with the AUDIT CTA.
3. Make 10 cold calls a day off a list of local businesses with weak sites (easy to find, most are).
4. Track one number: **teardowns delivered → calls booked → deals closed.** Fix whichever step leaks worst.

The offer is the lever. If conversion is still soft after this, it's almost never the script, it's the teardown not being sharp enough or the follow-up going cold.
