# Cold Call · Approach 4 · The Warm-Borrow (Referral)

**Mechanism being tested:** social proof + borrowed trust. You open on the back of a real client's name, so you're never fully a stranger. This is barely a cold call, which is exactly why it converts.
**Caller:** hired SDR / rep.
**Goal:** book the 15-minute call live, to show the teardown / mockup on screen.
**Destination offer:** free teardown → 15-min call → The 14-Day Booking Site.

Contrast vs the others: Approach 1 uses discomfort, Approach 2 uses delight, Approach 4 uses **belonging** ("someone you trust already vouched"). The test tells you which social force moves your market.

---

## The engine is the name, not the script (do this first)
This approach is only as strong as the referral behind it. The work happens before any dial:

1. **List your genuinely happy clients.** Even a handful is enough to start.
2. **Ask each for two things:**
   - Permission to use their name: *"Mind if I mention we worked together when I reach out to a couple other [industry] folks in town?"* Almost every happy client says yes.
   - A warm intro or two: *"Who's another [industry] owner around here you respect who could use a better site?"*
3. **Log exactly what you're allowed to say** for each client, so reps never overstate the connection. Three honest tiers:
   - **Tier 1 · Direct referral:** the client actually named this prospect. Strongest.
   - **Tier 2 · Name permission:** client is happy to be referenced, but didn't name this specific prospect.
   - **Tier 3 · Peer proof:** you worked with a similar business in the same town, no direct link.

Never claim a warmer tier than you have. The whole value is trust, and one exaggeration a prospect can smell kills it.

---

## The script

### Step 1 · The warm open (match it to your tier)

**Tier 1 — direct referral (use whenever you can):**
> Hi, is this [name]? Hey [name], I'm [rep] from Range of View. [Referrer] over at [their business] suggested I reach out to you, we redid their website and they've been really happy with how it's booking for them. They mentioned you as someone who'd actually appreciate good work. Got a minute?

**Tier 2 — name permission:**
> Hey [name], I'm [rep] from Range of View. We just did the website for [client] at [business] here in [city], and I'm reaching out to a couple other [industry] owners in town whose sites I think could book a lot more. You were on that short list. Got 30 seconds?

**Tier 3 — peer proof:**
> Hey [name], I'm [rep] from Range of View. We build websites for [industry] here in [city], we recently did one for [similar business] and it's booking really well for them. I called because I think yours could do the same. Quick 30 seconds?

*(The warmer the tier, the less you have to sell. Let the name do the work, then pause. Don't rush past it.)*

### Step 2 · The bridge (why them, specifically):
> The reason [Referrer] thought of you, honestly, is your [business] is clearly good but your site isn't pulling its weight. I looked before I called and [one specific leak: "there's no way to book without phoning" / "it's slow on mobile" / "the booking button's buried"]. That's the same thing we fixed for them.

*(Optional result graft:)*
> For [client] that one change took them from about [X] bookings a month to [Y]. Same town, same kind of business.

### Step 3 · Book to show them:
> Here's what I'd do, same as we did for [Referrer]: a quick 15-minute call where I put your site up on screen and show you the top few things costing you bookings, plus what the fix looks like. No obligation, and you keep the teardown either way. I've got [day] at [time] or [day] at [time], what's better?

**Lock it in:**
> Perfect. Best email for the invite? ... Great. I'll have your site pulled up and ready. And I'll tell [Referrer] I got hold of you. Talk [day].

*(That last line quietly reinforces the real relationship and gently raises the stakes on flaking, they don't want to no-show on a friend's referral.)*

## Objection handling

**"How do you know [Referrer]?"**
> We built their website, [timeframe] ago. [One true detail: "did their whole booking flow" / "the blue site with the online scheduler."] Give them a text if you want, they'll vouch. That's kind of the point, I'd rather you hear it from them than from me.

**"[Referrer] didn't tell me you'd call."**
> Fair, they may not have, I asked them who else in town does good work and your name came up. No pressure at all. But since I've got you, can I show you the same thing we did for them? 15 minutes.

**"We already have a website."**
> So did [Referrer]. The question was never whether they had one, it was whether it was booking people. Turned out it wasn't, and now it is. Let me show you where yours is leaking, same as I did for them. [day] or [day]?

**"How much did it cost them / cost me?"**
> Their project was in our usual range, $2k to $10k one time depending on scope. But for them it paid back fast in extra bookings, and that's exactly what I'll show you the math on. Free to look. [day] work?

**"I'm not really looking right now."**
> Totally fair. Can I at least send the same teardown we started [Referrer] with, and grab 15 minutes whenever's quiet for you? If the timing's wrong after that, no worries and I'll tell [Referrer] I tried.

**"Not interested."**
> No problem at all, I'll pass along that I reached you. If it ever changes, you know where we are. Take care, [name].

## After the call
- **Booked:** invite within 5 minutes. Bonus move, tell the referrer "reached out to [prospect], thanks for the intro." Keeps that client warm and primed to send more names.
- **No:** still thank the referrer. Every ask is also a chance to farm the next name.
- **Always** be feeding happy clients back into the referral engine. This approach compounds only if the name list keeps growing.

## What to log for the A/B/C test
```
Dials:                  ______
Tier used:              ____ T1 direct  /  ____ T2 permission  /  ____ T3 peer
Conversations:          ______
Calls booked:           ______
```
Log the tier per call, because the interesting finding isn't just "did Approach 4 win," it's *how warm does the borrow need to be to work.* If Tier 1 books at 40% and Tier 3 books like a normal cold call, the lesson is invest in getting named referrals, not just permission. Booked ÷ Conversations is the head-to-head number vs Approaches 1 and 2.
