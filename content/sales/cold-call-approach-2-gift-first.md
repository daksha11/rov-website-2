# Cold Call · Approach 2 · The Gift-First

**Mechanism being tested:** reciprocity + curiosity. You open by having *already built something for them.* No jolt, no problem-shaming. Nobody hangs up on "I made you a free thing."
**Caller:** hired SDR / rep.
**Goal:** book the 15-minute call live, to walk them through the mockup on screen.
**Destination offer:** free homepage mockup → 15-min call → The 14-Day Booking Site.

Contrast vs Approach 1: Approach 1 uses *discomfort* to earn attention. Approach 2 uses *delight.* The test tells you which one your market responds to.

---

## The catch that makes this work (read first)
The gift has to be real. Before you dial, someone at ROV has to have **actually rebuilt their homepage hero** (or a landing page) as a live test link. That means this is a **curated-list play, not a volume play.**

Operating model:
1. Pick **10–20 dream-fit local businesses** with weak-but-fixable sites.
2. Batch-build a fast homepage mockup for each (hero + one clear booking action + real photos pulled from their existing site). ROV can turn these out quickly, that speed is the whole point of the studio.
3. Host each on a private link.
4. *Then* call. Every dial has a real gift behind it.

Yes it's more work per lead. That's the trade: far higher connect-to-book rate on a small list beats low rates on a big one, and every mockup doubles as a portfolio piece even if they say no.

## The script

**Step 1 · The gift open (lead with the thing you made):**
> Hi, is this [name]? Hey [name], I'm [rep] from Range of View, we're a design studio here in [city]. I want to be upfront, I'm not selling you anything on this call. My team rebuilt your homepage this week, just as something we wanted to try, and it's sitting on a live link. I mostly called to send it to you. Got 20 seconds?

*(Almost nobody says no to this. If they're guarded: "No catch. We do these as portfolio pieces. I just figured the person it's actually of should get to see it.")*

**Step 2 · The curiosity swing (make them want to see it):**
> Here's what we changed. Your current site makes people [call / hunt around] to book. Ours puts one clear "book now" right up top, loads in about a second on a phone, and leads with your reviews. Same brand, same photos, it just actually turns visitors into bookings. It looks like *you*, just working harder.

*(Optional proof graft, if you have it:)*
> We did the same move for [similar business] and they picked up [Y] bookings a month off it. Same idea here.

**Step 3 · Book to reveal (don't just send the link):**
> I could email you the link, but it's way better if I put it on screen next to your current site and walk you through what changed and why. Fifteen minutes, no pitch, and the mockup's yours to keep either way. I've got [day] at [time] or [day] at [time], which works?

**Lock it in:**
> Perfect. Best email for the invite? ... Great, I'll have both versions pulled up side by side. Talk [day].

*(Why book instead of just sending: if you email the link cold, they glance at it and it dies. On a call you get to show the side-by-side, run the booking math, and read the room. The gift is the reason to meet, not the whole gift.)*

## Objection handling

**"Why would you build something for free?"**
> Two reasons, honestly. It's a portfolio piece for us either way, and it's the most honest way to show what we do, actual work on your actual business instead of a sales pitch. If you love it we'd talk about making it your real site. If not, no hard feelings, keep the ideas.

**"Just email me the link."**
> Happy to, but do me one favor, let's put 15 minutes on the calendar so I can show it to you next to your current site. That side-by-side is where it clicks. Otherwise it's just another link in your inbox. [day] or [day]?

**"How much would the real thing cost?"**
> The full rebuild runs $2k to $10k one time depending on scope. But the mockup's free regardless, and on the call I'll show you what the extra bookings are worth so you can see if the math even makes sense. No pressure to decide anything.

**"We already have a website / just paid someone."**
> Totally, and yours isn't bad. This is just a sharper take on the same brand, built to book more people. Worst case you get a free second opinion and some ideas you can hand to whoever built it. 15 minutes?

**"I don't have time."**
> Fair, that's why it's short and I did all the work already. What's your quietest slot, [day] morning or [day] afternoon? The mockup's not going anywhere.

**"Is this a scam / how'd you get my info?"**
> Totally reasonable to ask. You're a public [industry] in [city], that's the whole list, local shops we thought deserved a better site. No payment, no catch, I'm literally trying to give you something. You can look me and Range of View up before the call.

---

## After the call
- **Booked:** invite within 5 minutes, confirm you'll have both versions on screen. This lead is hot, treat the call like a real design review.
- **Wanted the link now:** send it, but always with "let's still grab 15 minutes so I can show you the part that matters." Then follow up once.
- **No:** the mockup was never wasted, file it as a portfolio piece. No chasing.

## What to log for the A/B/C test
```
Dials:                  ______
Conversations:          ______
"Send me the link" (no call):   ______   ← watch this leak
Calls booked:           ______
```
The failure mode here is people happily taking the free link and never booking. Track how many deflect to "just email it," because if that number is high, the fix is holding the reveal for the call harder, not building more mockups. Booked ÷ Conversations is the head-to-head number vs Approach 1 and 4.
