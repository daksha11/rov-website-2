## Buyers are drowning in new developments. Yours has to stand out before it stands up.

In a growing metro like Atlanta, a serious buyer or investor sees new projects constantly. Renders blur together. Names sound interchangeable. Most developments go to market looking like a spec sheet with a nice picture on top, and the buyer's memory of them fades by the next listing. The projects that sell fast are not always the best-built ones. They are the ones that felt like a place, early, before a single unit was finished.

We are Range of View Studios, an Atlanta creative studio. We name, brand, and launch new developments, taking a project from a site plan to a story people already want to be part of. Identity, renderings and narrative, marketing site, and launch content, built as one system by one team.

## A name is a promise, not a placeholder

Too many projects go to market under a working title that nobody chose with intention. But the name sets the tone everything else has to live up to. It shapes the identity, the story, the way the marketing site sounds. Get it right and the whole launch has a spine. Leave it as a placeholder and every later piece is quietly compensating for a weak center.

So naming is where we start. We choose the name with you, then build the visual system around it: the mark, the palette, the type, and the rules that keep it consistent from the hoarding on the fence to the closing packet in the buyer's hands. One identity, applied with discipline, so the project reads as a single confident thing everywhere it appears.

## Renderings are pictures. A story is why someone buys.

You have invested in renders, and they are probably beautiful. But a render on its own is just an image of a building. It does not tell a buyer what a morning here would feel like, who their neighbors are, why this corner of the city and not the one across the street. That gap between a picture and a life is where interest either ignites or drifts.

We take your renderings and give them a narrative. We frame the place as an experience a buyer can step into in their imagination, so the emotional decision, the one that actually drives a deposit, happens before the building exists. The renders stay. What we add is the reason they matter.

## A marketing site that sells, not just informs

Most development sites are brochures in disguise: specs, a gallery, a form. They answer questions, but they do not create desire. A launch site should do both. The story should lead, the imagery should carry the identity, and registering interest should be the easiest thing on the page. Because web and interactive is one of our four practice areas, we build that site in-house, in the same identity as the name and the story, so nothing is lost in a handoff.

## Launch as one coherent thing

When you launch, the market sees the project in fragments: a piece of film, a social post, a walk through the sales center, a billboard on the connector. If those fragments come from three different vendors, they never quite line up, and the launch feels assembled instead of authored. When they come from one team, they feel like one place speaking with one voice.

We build the launch content, the film, the motion, and the social, in the same brand as everything before it. Naming, identity, site, and content, drawn by the same hands. That is the real argument for using one studio instead of stitching specialists together: the story survives the whole journey intact.

## Honest about proof

We will be straight with you about experience. We work across sectors, and the mechanics that make a place feel inevitable, a strong name, a clear identity, a story that outruns the renders, transfer directly to real estate. Where a project needs proof specific to your asset class, we will show you the closest relevant work we have done and tell you honestly what carries over and what we would build fresh. No inflated claims, no borrowed case studies dressed up as our own.

## Built for how Atlanta grows

Atlanta is not one market. It is a dozen submarkets moving at different speeds, and a development that feels generic gets lost between them. We build for the specific place a project sits, its corridor, its buyers, its moment. We would rather make something that could only be this development, in this part of this city, and let that specificity be the reason a buyer remembers it a week later, when they are looking at the next ten.

Tell us about the project and where the planning stands. We will tell you what we would build and how we would sequence it.
