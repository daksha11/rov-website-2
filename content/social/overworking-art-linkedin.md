# Overworking Art — LinkedIn Post

Topic: "taking more time on creativity doesn't always make it better"
Voice: Suchet
Idea underneath: demo-itis / overworking a canvas / diminishing returns. Nuance: not anti-effort, know when the idea is done.
Status: locked

---

A song. A design. A painting. All three can be ruined the exact same way.

One more session.

Let me be clear first, because this gets misread. Some work takes years. Some ideas need a hundred passes and every one of them earns its place. That's real, and that's most of the great stuff.

But not everything.

Some ideas are done long before you're willing to admit it. And once an idea is exhausted, every extra session stops adding and quietly starts subtracting.

I noticed it first in music. A song that's alive after three sessions, and by session ten it's technically cleaner and somehow dead. You spend those last seven sessions changing things that were never broken. Sanding down the exact rough edges that were holding the feeling.

Producers have a word for this. Demo-itis. The scratch take, the messy first version, often has an energy the perfect one never gets back. Because the first pass captured something real, and everything after was just fixing.

You can polish the life right out of something.

The skill nobody teaches you is knowing which kind of project you're in. The one that needs another year, or the one that's been done for a week while you kept touching it.

Most of the time, the natural energy was the point. Protect it. Learn to hear when the idea has said what it came to say.

And then, the hardest part, stop.
