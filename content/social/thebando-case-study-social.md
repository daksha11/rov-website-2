## LINKEDIN CAPTION

First two sentences are the hook (all that shows before "...see more"):

A fried chicken restaurant on Atlanta's Westside grew its online orders by 689x. Not with ads, not with more traffic, just by rebuilding the website.

Here's the number that still gets us. Before we started, TheBando's online ordering page had pulled 132 views. Total. For a restaurant in one of the most competitive food cities in the country, the site existed but wasn't doing any work. It was a digital business card.

The problem wasn't traffic. The site already had plenty. The problem was that the one page that actually makes money, online ordering, was buried where almost nobody found it. So we rebuilt around it. Ordering became the front door instead of a footnote. We added dedicated menu pages, because people browse before they buy, and rebuilt the whole mobile flow for the phone in a hungry customer's hand.

139 days after launch, pulled straight from Wix Analytics:

Online ordering page views: 132 to 91,060, a 689x increase.
Total sessions: up 37.6%.
New menu pages: 139,398 views from pages that didn't exist before.
Paid ad spend to get there: zero.

The lesson isn't really about restaurants. Most local businesses don't have a traffic problem, they have a design problem. The visitors are already showing up. The site is just hiding the thing you want them to do.

In Terry's words: "Online orders went from a trickle to a flood, and people actually stay to read our story now."

Congrats to Terry and Darius at TheBando. If your site gets visitors but not orders, we're happy to tell you what it's hiding. Our inbox is open.
