A poster on the train. A YouTube video I wasn't really watching. A walk where I wasn't thinking about anything.

That's where my best ideas actually show up. Almost never at the desk.

For a long time I thought that meant I was doing it wrong. That real work should come from sitting down and forcing it. But the ideas never came when I was clenching. They came in the in-between moments, when I'd stopped trying.

Turns out there's a name for it. Incubation.

You load a problem into your head, a beat you're stuck on, a design direction that won't click, and then you walk away. And your brain keeps working on it in the background while you do something completely mundane. The answer shows up later, sideways, when you're not looking for it.

There's even an old saying for it. The three B's. Bed, bath, and bus. The three places ideas actually arrive.

I see the same thing in sports. The players who grip the hardest, who want it the most in the moment, choke. And the loose ones, the ones who look almost careless, play the best. Not because they care less. Because they're not strangling the thing they're trying to do.

Creativity works the same way. It doesn't come when you chase it. It comes when you loosen your grip.

So now I do the work differently. I load the problem on purpose, and then I let it go. I sit with the beat, then I leave. I trust that a poster on some train is already on its way.

Trying harder was never the answer. Trying, then getting out of the way, is.
