# The Taste Gap — LinkedIn Post

Topic: "the time will pass, do it anyway"
Voice: Suchet
Idea underneath: Ira Glass's taste gap + "the time passes anyway"
Status: locked

---

For years I didn't start web design, and it wasn't laziness.

It was taste.

I could already tell a good site from a bad one instantly. Which meant I also knew, before I ever opened the editor, that everything I built in my first year would be bad. And I would hate it. So why start something I already knew would embarrass me?

Ira Glass has a name for this. The gap. Your taste shows up years before your skill does, and it spends that whole time telling you your own work isn't good enough. Most people quit inside that gap. Not because they can't get better, but because being bad at something you can see clearly is its own kind of pain.

Here's the thing that finally moved me.

The years were going to pass anyway.

I could spend them not designing because I wasn't good yet, and end up right here, better taste, still no skill, still waiting. Or I could spend them shipping ugly sites I wasn't proud of, and come out the other side actually able to build the thing in my head.

Same years. The clock doesn't care which one I pick.

The taste that made my early sites painful is the exact same taste that makes my work good now. It was never the enemy. It was just early.

So I made the bad websites. A lot of them. And every one closed the gap a little.

The time is leaving either way. You might as well have something to show for it.
