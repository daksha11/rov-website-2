The one-paragraph truth

Agents are their own marketing department — and almost none of them are good at it.

Every agent is a 1099 small-business owner who only eats when a deal closes. The brokerage gives them less every year, the portals sell them shared leads at a brutal cost-per-close, and a 2024 law now forces them to sell their own value in writing before they do any work. Their best lead source — their own past clients — is almost never systematized. That gap is the whole business.

The wedge

Front-end brand, leaky back-end

Even Atlanta's top agents win on brand but fumble the funnel — dead websites, unanswered inquiries, no follow-up system. You don't just help beginners; you can improve the market leaders.

The language

Listings, not "leads"

They buy listing appointments and closings. Say "lead generation," "funnel," or "brand awareness" and you're instantly another vendor who doesn't get it.

The emotion

"Maybe it just isn't for me"

The pain isn't "I want more leads." It's bleeding money on fees and questioning the whole career. Sell relief from that, in their words.

The ecosystem

Who's who, and how money moves between them

Real estate is a web of independent contractors orchestrated around one transaction. Understanding who depends on whom tells you who to sell, and who will help pay for it.

Solo agent

1099 salesperson under a broker's license. Prospects, shows, negotiates, closes. Your core buyer.

nets 35–55% of gross

Team lead

Top producer who hires junior agents + a coordinator and feeds them leads for a split. Heavy marketing buyer.

takes 40–50% of team deals

Managing broker

Holds the license agents hang under; provides compliance, MLS, E&O insurance. Takes a cut of every deal.

split or cap per deal

Loan officer

The buyer's mortgage contact. Lives on agent referrals and co-markets constantly.

co-funds agent marketing ⭑

Transaction coordinator

Runs paperwork contract-to-close so the agent can sell. Signals an agent doing real volume.

~$300–500 / file

Title / escrow

Runs closing, verifies title, holds funds. Courts agents to stay on the preferred-vendor list.

paid at closing

Closing attorney (GA)

Georgia is an attorney-closing state — a real estate lawyer runs the closing, handles docs and escrow, disburses funds.

~$1,000+ / closing

Title insurance

Insures buyer + lender against title defects. Sold through the attorney/title company at closing.

~$1,000–1,800 one-time

Hazard insurance

Homeowner's policy the lender requires before funding. An insurance agent writes it — a co-marketing referral partner.

~$1,500/yr · buyer pays

Appraiser

Independent value opinion so the lender doesn't over-lend. Ordered by the lender, paid by the buyer.

~$500–600 · buyer pays

Home inspector

Evaluates physical condition during due diligence. Hired and paid by the buyer.

~$400–500 · buyer pays

Developer / builder

Land → entitlement → build → sell. Uses agents to move inventory. Treats marketing as a soft cost.

project-based budgets (Vertical #2)

Wholesaler / investor

The off-market cash economy. Runs big direct-outreach engines to find motivated sellers.

$5K–$30K assignment fees

The sphere

Past clients + network. The highest-ROI lead source (4–6× internet leads) — and almost never systematized.

free · un-systematized
The referral web: an agent must keep three parties happy — their sphere (referrals), their loan officer (borrower flow + co-marketing money), and their broker/team lead (license home + leads). The loan-officer relationship is a co-funding angle: they will literally help pay for an agent's marketing.

The pattern across all of them

Table stakes (sell the average agent)
  • Visible review volume as hero proof
  • A defined face or team brand
  • Consistent social at scale
  • Multi-path website lead capture
  • Neighborhood / local-SEO content
The wedge (sell everyone — even the top 1%)
  • Websites that under-convert the audience — the biggest, most repeatable gap
  • Broken follow-up (~50% of inquiries never answered)
  • No automated review engine
  • Video ignored (only 38% use it — 403% more inquiries)

The offer

Three angles you can lead with

One system, three doors in. Don't pitch all three cold. Diagnose the agent from their online presence, lead with the ONE angle that hits their pain, then expand once you've earned trust.

Angle 1

Build the Platform

How you look before a client ever calls.

  • Branded, converting website
  • Personal brand identity
  • Brand kit + media package

Lead here for: weak/template site, no brand, invisible online.

Angle 2

More Eyes, More Deals

How you get found and turn attention into listings.

  • SEO content → more traffic & eyes
  • Aerial footage + home walkthroughs → more sells & buys
  • The outcome: more listings

Lead here for: wants more listings, more volume, to be the local name.

Angle 3

Scalable Systems

How you keep every deal and buy back time.

  • CRM so no lead goes missing (referral + follow-up)
  • Automations that save time and make money

Lead here for: drowning in leads, bad follow-up, no time, feast-or-famine.

The trust play across all three: homework in the first line, value before the ask, and your first 30 days on us as the risk-killer. Land one angle in the free month, prove it, then expand into the other two. Never sell the whole system cold.
