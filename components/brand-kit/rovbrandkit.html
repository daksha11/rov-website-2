01 · Origin & Studio

Started in a server.
Built into a studio.

I started ROV in high school as a Discord. The idea was simple. Make a place where musicians actually talked to each other. Traded engineering tricks, broke down the business side, showed up for each other's work. It grew to 500 members, people from across the US and five continents, weekly events that happened because people wanted them to. That belief still runs everything we do today: the work gets better when humans are in the room.

College pulled things quiet. About a year and a half ago, my roommate and I rebuilt ROV as a creative studio. One that treats every output like it was authored, not assembled. Since then we've shipped work for The Bando (wing shop in ATL), Ayşegül İkna (Ponce City Market vendor), produced a fashion show at Ponce, built the site for ATL Tech Meet, and put out a long tail of packaging, posters, brand identities, film edits, and web platforms in between.

This past year we got deep on the dev side too. Full stack, AI agents, clean frontend, clean backend. Our point of view is flipped from most shops: in a world where making has never been easier, we spend the extra time where it actually matters, which is the human experience on the front of the screen. That's the edge we care about.

We're also building our own products. A music dashboard that cuts third party chaos out of an artist's workflow, so stems, notes, and approvals all live in one place. A brand kit generator that makes interactive kits like this one, so designers don't have to manually build the experience every time. More coming after that.

02 · Logo System

Four variants,
one mark.

The mark is hand drawn. Three angular panels folded like a fan, an eye at the center, and radial marks that pull from sun, stage, and signal. We ship four variants so the mark always reads right wherever it lands. Ember is the hero. Dark is for light grounds. White is for dark grounds. Paisley is the editorial moment, for when we want it to feel like a piece of fabric instead of a badge. Click any variant to grab the master PNG.

02 · Dark variant · for light grounds
03 · White variant · for dark grounds
04 · Paisley variant · special / editorial
Clear Space
1× all sides
Keep a clear space margin on every side at least equal to the height of the eye motif. Never crop the mark, rotate it past 5°, or lay it over busy imagery without a scrim. It has enough personality already. Give it room.
Minimum Sizes
Digital: 32px minimum height. The finer details hold down to that size. Print: 0.5in minimum. Below these sizes, switch to a monogram treatment. The eye motif compresses and loses its read.

08 · Brand Voice

Plainspoken
but precise.

We write the way we design. Nothing on the page that isn't earning its space. Warm, direct, quietly confident. If a sentence starts sounding like an agency brochure, it gets cut.

When we pitch
We build what the idea actually needs, not what the template suggests.
studio opening line
When we talk about craft
Dev is easy now. Taste is the hard part. We invest there.
thesis statement
When we talk about sound
We started in a music room. The room is bigger now. The ear didn't change.
founder note
DO
  • Write short, confident sentences. Cut filler before it earns rent.
  • Use italic or rust to add emphasis, not bold.
  • Reference the actual work. Name the client, name the problem.
  • Sound like a studio that ships, not a studio that theorizes.
DON'T
  • "Synergy", "leverage", "ecosystem", "best in class." Never.
  • Stack adjectives. Pick one and let it carry.
  • Over explain. Trust the reader to keep up.
  • Use AI buzzwords. Describe what it does, not what it is.
09 · Motion & Interaction

How the work
moves.

Motion stays restrained. Nothing bounces just to bounce. Four rules cover almost everything we ship.

01
Honest ease.
cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 0.2, 1) for state changes. Nothing elastic. Nothing that overshoots unless the brand is actually about overshoot.
02
Short and present.
Hovers between 150 and 250ms. Reveals between 500 and 600ms. Page transitions under 400ms. Anything longer is decoration.
03
One signal per moment.
A pulse, a slide, or a reveal. Never all three on the same element. Let the eye rest somewhere.
04
The rust leads.
If something moves to signal importance, it's rust. That color is the studio's heartbeat. Use it on pulses, hovers, and focus states, not backgrounds.
