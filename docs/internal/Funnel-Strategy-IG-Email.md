# Funnel Strategy: Instagram and Email

_Created 2026-07-31. Strategy only, no content produced. Companion to
`SEO-Topical-Authority-Plan.md`, which covers the on-site half._

## What this can and cannot do

**Instagram does not build topical authority.** Outbound social links are nofollowed and pass
no ranking signal. What Instagram and email do is generate **branded search** ("range of view
studios", "rov studios atlanta") and **direct traffic**, both of which Google does read as
demand signals. So this funnel amplifies the authority the site builds. It does not substitute
for it.

Judge the two halves on different numbers:

| Channel | Job | Judge it on |
|---|---|---|
| Site clusters | Earn the ranking | Impressions, position, AI citations |
| Instagram | Create demand and recognition | Branded search volume, saves, profile taps, link clicks |
| Email | Convert and retain attention | Reply rate, booked calls, list growth |

If Instagram is judged on rankings it will look like a failure. If it is judged on branded
search and list growth it will look like what it is.

## The single CTA

**Recommendation: the free visibility gap report.** Everything ends here. One destination,
one sentence, everywhere.

Why it wins:
- **It already exists.** The `rov-visibility-report` pipeline takes a business URL and produces
  a real gap report. No new deliverable to build, which is the usual reason funnels stall.
- **It captures an email**, unlike a booking link, so the 95% who are not ready today still
  enter the list instead of vanishing.
- **It proves the work instead of describing it.** A report showing their actual gaps is a
  stronger argument than any case study, and it pre-frames the paid engagement.
- **It matches the positioning.** The Full View argument is "money leaks in the gaps." The
  report literally shows them their gaps. The CTA and the pitch are the same idea.

**If you'd rather lead with the touchpoint audit** (the `/brand` wedge), the entire structure
below holds; swap the offer and the landing page and nothing else changes. The reason not to
lead with it yet is that the deliverable does not exist, and a CTA you cannot fulfill on
volume is worse than a plainer one you can.

**Do not** make the primary CTA "book a call." Keep it as the secondary action inside the
report follow-up, where intent is already established.

## Content pillars

Four pillars, mapped to the site clusters so nothing is written twice. Every post is a
compression of something that already exists on the site, or a probe for something that
should.

1. **The gap** (the `/brand` wedge, roughly 40%)
   The receipt that looks nothing like the website. The thank-you page that says "Success!".
   The 11pm email. This is the differentiated pillar and the most screenshot-able. Highest
   share rate, lowest competition, and it is the reason someone picks ROV over cheaper.

2. **Real numbers** (roughly 25%)
   What things cost, what they moved. Website pricing, the 689x, the $2,500 floor. Competitors
   hide numbers, which is exactly why publishing them travels.

3. **Local proof** (roughly 20%)
   Atlanta-specific: the Beltline, restaurant margins, the six verticals. This is the pillar
   national competitors cannot copy.

4. **The work itself** (roughly 15%)
   Process, craft, before-and-afters, the team. Builds the taste half of "taste plus plumbing."

## How Instagram hands off to email

The handoff is where most funnels break. Three rules:

1. **Link in bio points at a hub, never the homepage.** `/brand` or the gap-report landing
   page, depending on the post that drove them. The homepage answers no question anyone
   arrived with.
2. **Every post ends with the same CTA sentence.** Not a different clever line each time.
   Repetition is what makes a CTA get remembered and searched for later.
3. **The post is the trailer, the article is the film.** Do not write standalone Instagram
   content. Compress an existing article, then send them to it. That is what converts social
   attention into the on-site engagement that Google can actually see.

## The email sequence

The sequence is itself a demonstration of the service. If ROV's own lifecycle emails are
ugly or generic, the `/brand` pitch collapses. This is the single highest-stakes piece of
copy in the funnel.

**Trigger: someone requests the gap report.**

1. **The report, immediately.** No "thanks for signing up" delay. The thing they asked for,
   in the first email, looking like it came from the same company as the site.
2. **The biggest gap, explained.** Pick the worst finding in their report and explain what it
   costs them. Specific to them, not a newsletter.
3. **Proof.** The closest case study to their vertical. Restaurant gets TheBando, agent gets
   the follow-up piece.
4. **The wedge.** The gap argument in full: your brand stops at the logo, your customers
   don't. This is the email that sells the differentiator rather than the service.
5. **The plain ask.** What it costs, what happens next, one link to book. No urgency theater,
   no fake scarcity.

Then a low-frequency ongoing list, not a weekly newsletter nobody has time to write.

**Everything after email 1 should be reusable as blog content and vice versa.** The sequence
and the cluster feed each other or the whole thing is twice the work.

## Sequencing

Do not start this until the on-site work in `SEO-Topical-Authority-Plan.md` is further along.
Specifically:

- **Blocking:** the gap-report landing page and form must exist and be tested end to end.
  Driving traffic to a broken capture is the one genuinely costly mistake here.
- **Blocking:** decide the CTA. Everything downstream is written against it.
- **Should ship first:** the email funnel pricing post (T7/N1). It is the destination the
  strongest Instagram pillar points at, and it does not exist yet.
- **Nice to have first:** the local-visibility hub (T9), which is the natural landing page
  for gap-report traffic.

## Open questions for Andi

- Which platform sends the sequence? It determines what can be automated and what has to be
  manual.
- Is the gap report generated on demand per request, or in batches? The pipeline supports 1
  or 20 at a time; the promise in the CTA has to match the real turnaround.
- Instagram cadence you will actually sustain. Two posts a week that ship beats five that
  stall, and the content plan should be sized to the honest number.
- Does "Every business leaks money" get written? Five pages already link to it, it does not
  exist, and it is almost exactly the Full View argument. It would be the natural pillar
  article for the gap pillar and the report CTA.
