**Slide 1 — Hook**
> Your website looks fine.
> So why isn't it bringing in calls?

**Slide 2 — The story**
> An HVAC owner in Alpharetta got two website quotes.
>
> One said: pick a theme, done in an afternoon.
> The other listed words she'd never heard: Next.js, Tailwind, Vercel.
>
> She picked the one she recognized.
> Six months later, she wasn't showing up in search. At all.

**Slide 3 — The point**
> The software behind your site decides three things:
>
> 1. How fast it loads
> 2. Whether Google and AI search can read it
> 3. Whether it can grow without a rebuild
>
> The design is what you see. The stack is what gets found.

**Slide 4 — The stack, part 1**
> The 8 tools behind every site we ship:
>
> **Next.js** · the framework. Fast, crawlable, built to grow.
> **Tailwind CSS** · styling with no dead weight.
> **Figma** · the design, settled before a line of code.
> **shadcn/ui** · components we own outright. No black box.

**Slide 5 — The stack, part 2**
> **Framer Motion** · animation that looks expensive.
> **Supabase** · a real database, not a plugin.
> **Vercel** · every change previewed before it goes live.
> **Cursor** · AI that makes a good developer faster.
>
> No filler. If a client asks what we build with, this is the honest answer.

**Slide 6 — Myth 1**
> MYTH: "A no-code builder is basically the same as custom code."
>
> It looks the same on the surface.
> Underneath, templates ship code your site doesn't need, slow it down, and hide your content from search engines.
>
> That gap is most of what separates a $500 site from one built to be found.

**Slide 7 — Myth 2**
> MYTH: "AI website builders mean you don't need a developer."
>
> AI makes a good developer faster.
> It doesn't make the decisions that convert: what a customer sees first, where the CTA goes, how the site is structured for search.
>
> An AI site can look finished in an afternoon and still be invisible a year later.

**Slide 8 — The shift**
> Search is changing.
>
> Your customers aren't just typing into Google anymore. They're asking Perplexity and ChatGPT: "who's a good roofer near me?"
>
> Getting picked by those answers depends on how your site is built. Not just what it says.

**Slide 9 — The one question**
> You don't need to understand the code.
>
> You need one question when comparing quotes:
>
> "What do you build with, and why?"
>
> If the answer is "our platform" instead of a named technology, that's a template with a markup.
