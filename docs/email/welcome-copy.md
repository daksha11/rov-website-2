# Welcome email copy

Audience: business card scans (`ROV Business Leads`, SQRrEK) and blog / web signups
(`ROV web leads`, WGRd8Q + `Email List`, YiZ3cG).
Job: brand awareness, soft prompt to book a call.
Template: `ROV Welcome` in Klaviyo, ID `VDXcUH`.

One template, two openers. Run it as two flows off the same template and swap
only the opener line, so the card version can reference the meeting.

---

## The angle

**Your brand doesn't clock out.**

This is the spine of the email and it should be the spine of the whole welcome
flow. It works for three reasons:

1. **It matches the image.** A phone off the hook is a call arriving when nobody
   is at the desk. The art and the argument are finally saying the same thing.
2. **It is the Full View argument without the jargon.** "Money leaks in the gaps"
   is true but abstract. "Your brand is still working at 11pm and you are not
   watching it" is the same claim, and the reader can picture it.
3. **It lands on the sharpest part of the differentiator.** Per `company.md`, the
   Kept moment is where you separate hardest. Anyone can send a review request.
   Almost nobody sends one that looks like it came from a company with taste.

## Pull quote

---

## Body

> It is still working at 11pm. In the auto-reply nobody proofread. In the receipt.
> In the thank-you page that just says "Success!"
>
> Those are brand moments. Most of them were built by someone who was not thinking
> about your brand at all.
>
> Design studios hand over a beautiful site and stop there. Automation shops build
> something that works and looks like a parking garage receipt.
>
> We do both. The site, the reply, the email at 11pm. One company all the way down,
> and most of it running without anyone remembering to run it.

Four short paragraphs, each one a turn:

- **One** makes it concrete. Three specific failures, no adjectives. "Success!"
  is the single most recognizable bad-brand artifact on the internet and costs
  you two words.
- **Two** reframes those failures as brand, which is the move the entire pitch
  depends on. If the reader accepts this sentence, they have accepted the offer.
- **Three** names the gap in the market without naming a competitor.
- **Four** is the offer. "One company all the way down" is the slicker version of
  the one-liner and it earns the rhythm the three setup paragraphs built.

Do not soften the parking garage receipt. It is specific, slightly funny, and it
makes the reader picture their own last automated email.

## What is deliberately not here

- **No pricing.** The $2,500 floor is right to publish, wrong in a first touch to
  someone who has not asked what anything costs.
- **No case study.** This email is one idea. Proof dilutes it. Save it for email two.
- **No urgency, no scarcity, no discount ladder.**
- **No cadence promise.** Your funnel doc already names "a weekly newsletter nobody
  has time to write" as the failure mode. Do not commit to one here.
